# Wiki authoring

- Always include a References section when creating a wiki page.
- Cite sources near the definitions, theorems, algorithms, complexity claims, and historical claims they support. Prefer primary sources, textbooks, and official documentation, and use working links when available.
- Verify source content before citing it. Distinguish worked examples and derivations from quotations; do not invent references or page numbers.
- Follow the existing wiki front matter and use `relative_url` for links between wiki entries.
- Include a historical narrative that explains the original practical or theoretical problem, why the concept or method was introduced, and how later developments changed its use or understanding. Connect this history to the mathematical explanations and worked examples; do not substitute an isolated attribution or a list of dates for the narrative. Use verified sources, distinguish retrospective accounts from original publications, and avoid unsupported priority claims.
- Teach for familiarity with the concept, not only formal correctness. Explain what problem it solves, why its assumptions matter, what its symbols mean, and how it connects to ideas readers already know.
- Explain unfamiliar terms before using them in an explanation or derivation, with simple examples. Link to prerequisite wiki pages when a concept needs more explanation, while providing enough local context for readers to follow the current page.
- Include substantial intuition, step-by-step worked examples, useful visualizations, and counterexamples to common misconceptions. Keep examples and diagrams mathematically consistent and explain how to read each visual.
- Add interactive examples when they materially help readers explore the concept. Give readers meaningful controls, explain the changing results, handle invalid or degenerate inputs, support keyboard use, and retain a useful explanation when JavaScript is unavailable.
- Include practice problems across four types: hand calculation, questions requiring interpretation or strategic thinking, abstract or logical reasoning, and practical applications. Provide hints and worked solutions that readers can reveal after attempting the problems. Scale the number and difficulty to the subject rather than using repetitive filler.
- Include helpful learning resources with verified links and explain what each resource helps readers learn. Distinguish original exercises and illustrative examples from sourced claims.
- Verify example arithmetic, solutions, diagram labels, interactive behavior, and rendered math. Preserve historical content when expanding explanations unless a correction is justified by evidence.
